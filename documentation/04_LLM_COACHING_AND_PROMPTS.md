# Generative AI Coaching Layer: Prompts & Guardrails

This document describes the large language model (LLM) coaching layer that interprets biomechanical telemetry into athletic guidance.

---

### 1. Design Philosophy: Strict Guardrails vs. Hallucination

In clinical and sports biomechanics, automated feedback systems must obey strict safety and epistemic constraints. Unconstrained language models tend to:
- Invent phantom gait phases (e.g. diagnosing "overpronation" when foot eversion was never measured).
- Offer medical or injury diagnoses (e.g. diagnosing "runner's knee" or "patellar tendinopathy").
- Make value judgments ("you are a bad runner").
- Prescribe random or unsupported gym exercises.

To prevent this, the LLM is bound by **ten strict negative constraints** and instructed to cleanly decouple **Observation** from **Interpretation**.

### 2. Model Routing & Fallback Architecture

The system communicates with the **Groq Cloud API** using low-latency inference. Because specific models on Groq accounts may vary by tier or deprecation cycle, the backend uses a sequential model fallback strategy:


### 3. Exact System Prompt

Below is the verbatim system prompt passed to the model:

```text
You are an AI running biomechanics feedback assistant.

You receive structured measurements generated by a computer-vision biomechanics pipeline.
Your job is to explain ONLY the information supplied.

STRICT RULES:
1. Do not invent measurements.
2. Do not invent gait phases.
3. Do not diagnose injuries or medical conditions.
4. Do not claim the athlete is a good or bad runner.
5. Do not say a movement is universally correct or incorrect.
6. Do not recommend specific exercises unless the input explicitly provides evidence.
7. Clearly separate observation from interpretation.
8. Use cautious language: "possible deviation", "differs from the reference", "may indicate", "consider focusing on".
9. Keep the feedback concise and understandable.

Structure your response with EXACTLY these four sections:

WHAT WE OBSERVED:
<your text>

POSSIBLE INTERPRETATION:
<your text>

COACHING FOCUS:
<your text>

OVERALL SUMMARY:
<your text>
```

### 5. Robust Section Parsing Engine

LLMs occasionally format section headers with slight variations (e.g., `**WHAT WE OBSERVED:**`, `### What We Observed:`, or numbered prefixes). The parsing engine uses case-insensitive regular expressions with lookaheads to extract the contents cleanly:

```python
import re

def extract_section(text, sec_name):
    pattern = (
        rf"(?:^|\n)\s*(?:#+\s*)?(?:\*\*)?{sec_name}(?:\*\*)?:?\s*\n?"
        rf"(.*?)(?=(?:\n\s*(?:#+\s*)?(?:\*\*)?"
        rf"(?:WHAT WE OBSERVED|POSSIBLE INTERPRETATION|COACHING FOCUS|OVERALL SUMMARY)"
        rf"(?:\*\*)?:?)|$)"
    )
    m = re.search(pattern, text, re.IGNORECASE | re.DOTALL)
    if m:
        val = m.group(1).strip()
        return re.sub(r"^\*+\s*", "", val).strip()
    return ""
```

#### Generated Coaching Output
- **WHAT WE OBSERVED:**
  The video footage provided 90 frames at 30 FPS with high detection quality (95%). The athlete demonstrated a mean right knee flexion of 145.2° and left knee flexion of 154.5°. While both limbs fall within the broader reference range (140.0°–165.0°), the right knee deviates by -9.8° relative to the reference mean, exhibiting an asymmetry of approximately 9.3° compared to the left limb.

- **POSSIBLE INTERPRETATION:**
  The lower flexion angle on the right knee may indicate reduced knee flexion during the swing phase or early stance contact. This bilateral discrepancy differs from the reference symmetry and may reflect a tendency to maintain a stiffer right leg through mid-stride.

- **COACHING FOCUS:**
  Consider focusing on bilateral balance and active right-leg heel recovery. Drills emphasizing dynamic knee drive and rhythmic stride symmetry (such as high-knee skips and step-over drills) may help encourage symmetrical lower-limb mechanics.

- **OVERALL SUMMARY:**
  The runner displays solid foundational movement with overall metrics within acceptable envelopes, with a possible asymmetry in right knee flexion worth monitoring during training.
